Note that any field extension \(K/F\) is an \(F\)-algebra and hence an \(F\)-vector space. Throughout this section, let \(K/F\) be a field extension.

!!! definition "Degree and Finite Extension"

    The *degree* of the extension \(K/F\) is the dimension \(\dim_F K\), and is denoted by \([K:F]\). If \([K:F]<\infty\), we say that the extension is *finite*.

Note that the degree is always positive, since \(F\) is a subspace of \(K\). We first observe that the degree is multiplicative.

!!! proposition

    Let \(L/K/F\) be extensions of fields. We have \([L:F] = [L:K][K:F]\), and in particular the extension \(L/F\) is finite if and only if both extensions \(L/K, K/F\) are finite.

!!! proof

    Let \((\alpha_i)_{i\in I}\) be an \(F\)-basis of \(K\) and \((\beta_j)_{j\in J}\) be a \(K\)-basis of \(L\). We claim that the set \((\alpha_i\beta_j)_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\) is an \(F\)-basis of \(L\).

    First, every element of \(L\) is a finitely supported sum \(\sum_{j}b_j\beta_j\) where \(b_j\in K\), and each \(b_j\) is a finitely supported sum \(\sum_{i}a_{ij}\alpha_i\) where \(a_{ij}\in F\). Thus every element of \(L\) is a finitely supported sum \(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}\beta_j\). If a finitely supported sum \(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\alpha_i\beta_j\) is zero, then as \((\beta_j)_{j\in J}\) is \(K\)-linearly independent, we have \(\sum_ia_{ij}\alpha_i = 0\) for all \(j\in J\). Since \((\alpha_i)_{i\in I}\) is \(F\)-linearly independent we have \(a_{ij} = 0\) for all \(i,j\). Thus the claim is proved.

The finite extensions are closely related to algebraic extensions.

!!! definition "Zero and Algebraic Extension"

    For any \(\alpha\in K\), there is unique morphism of \(F\)-algebras \(F[x]\to K\) sending \(x\) to \(\alpha\), and we denote this by \(f\mapsto f(\alpha)\). If \(f(\alpha) = 0\), we say that \(\alpha\) is a *zero* of \(f\). An element \(\alpha\) of \(K\) is called *algebraic* over \(F\) if there is a nonzero polynomial \(f\in F[x]\) having \(\alpha\) as its zero. The extension \(K/F\) is called *algebraic* if every element of \(K\) is algebraic over \(F\).

!!! definition

    Let \(X\) be a subset of \(K\). There is a smallest subextension of \(K/F\) that contains \(X\), called the *subextension generated by \(X\)*, and denoted by \(F(X)\). When \(X = \{x_1,...,x_n\}\) is a finite set, we also denote this by \(F(x_1,\cdots,x_n)\).

Let us check well-definedness.

- \(K/F\) is a subextension of itself containing \(X\), and intersection of such subextensions is again such a subextension.
- The extension \(F(X)\) consists of rational functions with variables in \(X\); formally, there is universal morphism of \(F\)-algebras \(F[X]\to K\) sending each \(x_i\) to \(x_i\in K\), and the image (a subring of \(K\)) is also denoted by \(F[X]\) by abuse of notation. Then \(\Frac(F[X])\) is a subfield of \(K\) consisting of rational functions of variables in \(X\), coefficients in \(F\) and with nonzero denominators. This is clearly the smallest subextension.

!!! lemma

    Let \(\alpha\) be an element of \(K\). TFAE:

    - \(\alpha\) is algebraic over \(F\).
    - The extension \(F(\alpha)/F\) is finite.
    - \(\alpha\) is contained in some finite subextension \(E/F\) of \(K/F\).
