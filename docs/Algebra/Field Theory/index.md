The theory of fields is not an algebraic theory (in mathematical logic or in category theory). Therefore, these categories behave very differently with other categories in algebra, such as the categories \(\Grp,\CRing\), etc., even if we fix a characteristic. There are usually many different morphisms of fields, and in particular the concept of algebraic / separable closures are not 'universal' in the sense of category theory; there can be many different embeddings of an algebraic extension to the algebraic closure. Field theory deals with these phenomena in detail.

Since fields can be thought of the most simple cases of (commutative) rings, where only ideals are \((0)\) and \((1)\), the theory is utilized in many different fields of mathematics. In particular, a field represents a point in algebraic geometry, and hence the theory of fields are extensively used and generalized in the context of algebraic geometry (and also in algebraic number theory).
